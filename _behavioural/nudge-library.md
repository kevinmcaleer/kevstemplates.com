Ready tactics, organised by the barrier they address. Find your barrier, pick two or three, filter with APEASE.

⚠️ Nudges have real but **modest** effect sizes and cannot rescue a badly designed process. Use them to amplify a sound design, not to compensate for a broken one. See the limits section in [EAST — Easy Attractive Social Timely](/behavioural-change/east-easy-attractive-social-timely/).

---

## 🔧 Physical Opportunity — "there isn't time / access / kit"

The highest-yield category, and the most ignored.

1. **Make it the default.** Pre-selected option, pre-populated form, auto-created record. The strongest single lever available.
2. **Count the clicks and remove three.** Literally count. Every step costs adoption.
3. **Cut the form.** Halve the fields. Then ask which of the remainder anyone ever reads.
4. **Auto-populate from what's already known** — calendar, previous entry, the record it's linked to.
5. **Switch off the old route.** With a hard date and a named owner. Parallel running kills adoption.
6. **Move the task to where the person already is** — phone rather than desktop, in the meeting rather than after it.
7. **Take something away.** Remove a report, a meeting, an approval. Publicise what you removed — it buys enormous goodwill.
8. **Protect the time explicitly.** A calendar block that a manager defends is worth more than an instruction.
9. **Pre-fill the hard part** and let people edit rather than create.
10. **Put the tool in the workflow, not in a separate system.** Every context switch loses people.

## 📚 Capability — "I don't know how"

11. **One-page job aid at the point of use** — not a 40-page manual, not a portal.
12. **Two-minute video showing one task**, filmed on a phone by a peer, not a studio.
13. **Floorwalkers in the first two weeks.** Physically present, not a ticket queue.
14. **Practice on real work**, not sample data. Transfer from training environments is poor.
15. **A named human to ask.** Named, not a mailbox. "Ask Priya" beats a helpdesk.
16. **Checklist for the complex path**, so nobody has to hold it in their head.
17. **Teach one thing at a time**, in the week it's needed — not everything in one session six weeks early.
18. **Peer teaching.** People learn faster from someone one step ahead than from an expert.

## 👥 Social Opportunity — "nobody round here does this"

19. **Publish descriptive norms** — "78% of your team did this last week." Only when the number is good; otherwise use a trend. See [Social Norms and Influence](/behavioural-change/social-norms-and-influence/).
20. **Local comparison** — team vs site average, not individual vs company.
21. **Name real people doing it well**, with what they got out of it.
22. **Public specific commitment** made to named colleagues, with a date.
23. **Visible champions** distributed across teams, not centralised. [Champion Network Playbook](/behavioural-change/champion-network-playbook/)
24. **Leaders visibly doing it** — the single strongest social signal available. [Leader Behaviour Contract](/behavioural-change/leader-behaviour-contract/)
25. **Make the work visible** — shared boards, open queues. Invisible behaviour can't become normal.
26. **Peer review as routine**, so the behaviour has an audience.
27. **Stop accepting the old format.** A manager who accepts the old spreadsheet "just this once" undoes weeks of work.

## 🧠 Reflective Motivation — "I don't think it's worth it"

28. **Reframe from organisational benefit to personal benefit.** "Saves you the Friday reconciliation" beats "improves data quality."
29. **Show the real cost of the current way** in hours, in their own numbers, collected by them.
30. **Involve them in design.** The most reliable commitment generator there is.
31. **One vivid story** beats a deck of charts. A recording, a customer complaint, a real Tuesday.
32. **Answer "why now" honestly.** If the honest answer is a regulatory deadline, say so. Invented urgency is detected.
33. **Concede the downside first.** "This adds four minutes per call" said by you is credibility; said by them, it's an ambush.
34. **Let them articulate the case in their own words.** Two minutes of them explaining it beats an hour of being told.

## ⚡ Automatic Motivation — "I do the old thing without thinking"

35. **Attach to an existing cue.** End of a call, closing a ticket, start of stand-up. Never invent a new cue. [Habit Loop and Behavioural Drift](/behavioural-change/habit-loop-and-behavioural-drift/)
36. **Implementation intentions** — get people to write "when X, I will Y." 90 seconds, robust effect.
37. **Prompt at the exact moment**, in the tool, not by email the day before.
38. **Remove the old cue.** If the old spreadsheet still opens on login, the old habit still fires.
39. **Immediate feedback.** Something visibly happens within seconds of doing it right.
40. **Same-day recognition** beats quarterly awards for habit formation. Small, fast, certain.
41. **Make the first instance trivially small.** "Just log one call this week."
42. **Streaks and visible continuity** — but only where they won't create dishonest reporting.

## 🕐 Timing

43. **Use the disruption window.** A rollout, a move, a new role, a return from leave — habits are already loose. Land the new behaviour then.
44. **Bring one benefit into week one**, however small. Future benefits are heavily discounted.
45. **Plan a deliberate week-8 re-intervention.** That's where drift bites and where everyone has stopped paying attention.

---

## Choosing

Pick **one from the barrier category** you diagnosed, plus **one environmental** (make it easy), plus **one social**. Three well-chosen and well-executed beats twelve announced.

<div class="bct-callout bct-tip" markdown="1">
**The two-question test for any tactic**

1. Would this still work if nobody read the emails?
2. Would I be comfortable if the people affected could see exactly why we're doing it?

If no to (1), it's comms not intervention. If no to (2), don't do it.
</div>
