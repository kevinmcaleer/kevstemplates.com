# Knowledge Management

Knowledge management is the deliberate effort to create, capture, organise, share and reuse what people in an organisation know. On programmes and projects it makes sure hard-won expertise survives team changes, supplier changes and the move from project to business as usual.

---

## Why It Matters

Programmes run for years, and people come and go. Without knowledge management:

- Decisions are made again because nobody remembers why they were made the first time
- The same mistakes are repeated from project to project
- A single person becomes the only one who understands a critical system
- Handover to operations relies on goodwill and memory
- New starters take months to become effective

Good knowledge management turns individual experience into organisational capability.

---

## Types of Knowledge

| Type | Description | Examples | How to Manage It |
|------|-------------|----------|------------------|
| **Explicit** | Written down and easy to share | Procedures, designs, runbooks, contracts | Document management, version control, repositories |
| **Tacit** | Held in people's heads, learned by doing | Judgement, workarounds, who to call, why things are the way they are | Shadowing, mentoring, storytelling, communities of practice |
| **Embedded** | Built into processes, tools and culture | Workflows, system configuration, team habits | Process mapping, configuration management |
{:class="table table-single table-narrow"}

Most of the value, and most of the risk, sits in **tacit knowledge**. It cannot be handed over in a document alone, which is why [Knowledge Transfer](/programmes/knowledge_transfer) uses a mix of methods.

## The Knowledge Management Cycle

<div class="mermaid">

    A[Identify] --> B[Capture]
    B --> C[Organise]
    C --> D[Share]
    D --> E[Apply]
    E --> F[Review]
    F --> A
    classDef blue fill:#108BB9,stroke:none,color:#fff
    class A,B,C,D,E,F blue
</div>

| Stage | Activities | Outputs |
|-------|-----------|---------|
| **Identify** | Work out what knowledge is critical and who holds it | Knowledge map, single points of failure |
| **Capture** | Write it down, record it, or pair people to pass it on | Documents, recordings, lessons, KT sessions |
| **Organise** | Store it where people can find it, with an owner | Structured repository, index, metadata |
| **Share** | Push it to the people who need it | Briefings, communities of practice, training |
| **Apply** | Use it in planning and decision making | Better estimates, fewer repeated mistakes |
| **Review** | Check it is still current, retire what is not | Updated or archived content |
{:class="table table-single table-narrow"}

## Knowledge Management, Information Management and Knowledge Transfer

| Discipline | Focus | Typical Question |
|------------|-------|------------------|
| **Information Management** | Records, documents and data: storage, access, retention | "Where is the latest version of the design?" |
| **Knowledge Management** | The organisation's know-how and how it grows over time | "What have we learned about delivering this kind of change?" |
| **Knowledge Transfer** | Moving specific knowledge from one person or team to another | "Can the support team run this service without the project team?" |
{:class="table table-single table-narrow"}

See [Information Management](/programmes/information_management) and [Knowledge Transfer](/programmes/knowledge_transfer) for more detail.

## Knowledge Management Practices

| Practice | What It Is | When to Use It |
|----------|-----------|----------------|
| **[Lessons learned](/projects/lessons_learned)** | Structured capture of what went well and badly | End of each stage and project, after major events |
| **After action reviews** | Short, informal reviews straight after an event | After go-lives, incidents, workshops |
| **Knowledge transfer** | Planned handover of knowledge between people or teams | Leavers, project to BAU handover, supplier change |
| **Communities of practice** | Groups that share expertise on a topic | Recurring disciplines (e.g. testing, PMO, architecture) |
| **Knowledge repositories** | Well-organised, searchable stores of documents | Throughout the programme |
| **Mentoring and coaching** | Experienced people developing others | Building long-term capability |
| **Expert directories** | A list of who knows what | Large or distributed programmes |
| **Storytelling and case studies** | Narrative accounts of what happened and why | Passing on context and judgement |
{:class="table table-single table-narrow"}

## Building a Knowledge Management Approach

A programme's knowledge management approach can sit within the information management strategy or stand on its own. It should cover:

1. **Critical knowledge** - which knowledge the programme cannot afford to lose
2. **Knowledge holders** - who holds it, including suppliers and contractors
3. **Single points of failure** - people or teams who are the only source of key knowledge
4. **Capture methods** - how knowledge will be captured and by when
5. **Storage and access** - where it lives and who can see or update it
6. **Transfer triggers** - events that start a knowledge transfer (leavers, contract end, go-live)
7. **Roles** - who owns knowledge management and who is accountable for each area
8. **Measures** - how you will know it is working

---

## Roles and Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibilities |
|------|------------------|
| **Programme / Project Manager** | Makes sure knowledge risks are identified and knowledge transfer is planned and resourced |
| **Business Change Manager** | Makes sure operational teams receive the knowledge they need to realise benefits |
| **PMO** | Maintains repositories, runs lessons learned, tracks KT progress |
| **Knowledge holders** | Share what they know, keep documentation current |
| **Line managers** | Release time for knowledge sharing, plan succession |
{:class="table table-single table-narrow"}

---

## Measuring Success

- Number of single points of failure identified and removed
- Percentage of critical topics with an up-to-date owner and documentation
- KT plans completed before people or suppliers leave
- Lessons applied (not just recorded) on later projects
- Time for new starters to become effective
- Repeat incidents caused by missing knowledge

---

## Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | Consequence | How to Avoid It |
|---------|-------------|-----------------|
| Leaving it to the end | Knowledge walks out of the door with leavers | Identify knowledge risks at the start and plan transfer early |
| Documents only | Tacit knowledge is never passed on | Combine documents with shadowing and paired work |
| No owner | Content goes out of date and is not trusted | Give every critical document an owner and review date |
| Lessons recorded but not used | Same mistakes repeated | Review lessons at project start-up and stage planning |
| No time allowed | Holders are too busy to share | Put KT activities in the plan with real effort estimates |
{:class="table table-single table-narrow"}
