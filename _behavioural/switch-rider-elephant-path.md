Chip and Dan Heath's model. Its power is **communicative**: it's the framework that makes an executive audience nod. Use it in the room; use [COM-B and the Behaviour Change Wheel](/behavioural-change/com-b-and-the-behaviour-change-wheel/) behind the scenes.

---

## The metaphor

A rider sits on an elephant on a path.

- **The Rider** = rational, analytical mind. Looks strong, is easily exhausted and prone to over-analysis. Wants direction.
- **The Elephant** = emotion, instinct, habit. Six tonnes of it. Wins every fight with the Rider. Wants motivation.
- **The Path** = the environment. Change the path and you barely need the other two.

> **What looks like resistance is often lack of clarity. What looks like laziness is often exhaustion. What looks like a people problem is often a situation problem.**

That last line is the single most useful sentence you can say to a senior leader who is frustrated with their people.

---

## Direct the Rider

**Follow the bright spots.** Find where the new behaviour is *already happening* and study it. Someone in your organisation is already doing this well with the same constraints. Clone them rather than importing an outside model — it's faster, cheaper and far more persuasive.

**Script the critical moves.** Don't say "be more customer-focused." Say "call the client back the same day, even if you don't have the answer yet." Ambiguity is the enemy of change. See [Target Behaviour Definition](/behavioural-change/target-behaviour-definition/).

**Point to the destination.** A "destination postcard" — a vivid picture of the near-term future. Not a KPI. *"By Christmas, nobody on this team spends their Friday afternoon reconciling spreadsheets."*

## Motivate the Elephant

**Find the feeling.** Analysis rarely changes behaviour. Show, don't tell: a customer complaint read aloud, a video of someone's actual Tuesday, the real cost in hours. One concrete story beats a deck of charts.

**Shrink the change.** Make the first step embarrassingly small so it can't be refused. "Just log one call this week." Small wins build the momentum that big goals kill.

**Grow your people.** Give an identity, not just a task. "We're the team that fixes things properly" is more durable than any incentive.

## Shape the Path

**Tweak the environment.** Defaults, layout, forms, calendar invites, system settings. Change the path and behaviour follows without persuasion. This is where the cheapest wins live.

**Build habits.** Attach the new behaviour to an existing cue. See [Habit Loop and Behavioural Drift](/behavioural-change/habit-loop-and-behavioural-drift/).

**Rally the herd.** Behaviour is contagious. Make the right behaviour visible. See [Social Norms and Influence](/behavioural-change/social-norms-and-influence/).

## The three-minute exec pitch

Fill this in and say it out loud:

> **The Rider is unclear.** People don't know what "good" looks like day to day. So we're scripting three specific moves: `________`, `________`, `________`.
>
> **The Elephant isn't moved.** The case is rational and nobody feels it. So we're `________` — bringing the real cost into the room.
>
> **The Path is against us.** Right now the old way is genuinely easier. So we're `________` — changing the default, removing `___` steps, and making the new way the only route into the Monday meeting.
>
> **And the biggest single thing that shapes the path is what you and your leadership team visibly do.**

That last line is why this framework is worth using with executives: it lands the accountability without an accusation.
