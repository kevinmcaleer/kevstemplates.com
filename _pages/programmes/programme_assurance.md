# Programme Assurance

Programme assurance gives the Senior Responsible Owner (SRO) and programme board confidence that the programme will deliver its benefits, and an early warning when it will not. It is planned at the start, sized to the programme's risk, and kept independent of the people doing the delivery.

---

## What is Programme Assurance?

<div class="alert alert-info">
<i class="fas fa-info-circle mr-2"></i>
<strong>Definition:</strong> Programme assurance is the set of independent checks and reviews that tell decision-makers whether the programme is set up to succeed, is on track to realise its benefits, and is being run in line with its governance.
</div>

Assurance at programme level looks different from project assurance:

| Aspect | Project Assurance | Programme Assurance |
|--------|-------------------|---------------------|
| **Main question** | Will the project deliver its products to time, cost and quality? | Will the programme realise its benefits and reach the blueprint? |
| **Focus** | Outputs and plans | Outcomes, benefits, dependencies and strategic fit |
| **Who it serves** | Project board and executive | SRO, programme board and sponsoring group |
| **Scope** | One project | All projects, their dependencies and the business change |
| **Timing** | Stage boundaries | Tranche boundaries, plus continuous monitoring |
{:class="table table-single table-narrow"}

For the general principles of assurance, see the [Assurance best practice guide](/best_practice/assurance).

## The Three Lines Model

Assurance is layered. Each line adds independence, and the board should know which line each piece of evidence comes from.

| Line | Who | What they do | Example |
|------|-----|--------------|---------|
| **First line** | Programme manager, project managers, programme office | Day-to-day control and self-assessment | Monthly [Programme Healthcheck](/programmes/programme_healthcheck), RAID reviews |
| **Second line** | PMO, portfolio office, finance, risk and quality functions | Oversight against standards, independent of delivery | Portfolio assurance review, financial controls check |
| **Third line** | Internal audit, external reviewers, gateway review teams | Fully independent review that reports to the board or sponsor | Gate review, internal audit of benefits tracking |
{:class="table table-single table-narrow"}

## Planning Assurance

Write an assurance plan during programme definition and agree it with the SRO. Size it to the programme's risk: a high-value, high-risk programme needs more third-line reviews than a low-risk internal one.

<div class="mermaid">

    B --> C[Schedule reviews<br/>by tranche]
    C --> D[Carry out<br/>reviews]
    D --> E[Report findings<br/>to board]
    E --> F[Track actions<br/>to closure]
    F --> C

    class A,B,C,D,E,F blue
</div>

An assurance plan should cover:

- **Scope**: which areas are reviewed (benefits, governance, finance, delivery, business change, suppliers)
- **Schedule**: reviews mapped to tranche ends and key decisions
- **Reviewers**: who carries out each review and how independent they are
- **Integration**: how programme reviews link to project assurance, so the same team is not reviewed twice in a month
- **Reporting**: who receives findings and how actions are tracked
- **Budget**: time and cost for reviews, including external reviewers

## Gate and Tranche Reviews

Gate reviews happen at decision points. At programme level these usually fall at the end of definition and at each tranche boundary.

| Review | When | Key questions |
|--------|------|---------------|
| **Strategic assessment** | Before the programme is approved | Is there a clear strategic need? Is a programme the right vehicle? |
| **Programme definition review** | End of definition | Are the blueprint, benefits map, business case and plan coherent and realistic? |
| **Tranche end review** | End of each tranche | Were the tranche's capabilities delivered? Are benefits starting to appear? Should the next tranche go ahead? |
| **Readiness for service review** | Before major transitions | Is the business ready to adopt the new capability and support it? |
| **Benefits realisation review** | After major transitions and at closure | Are benefits being realised as forecast? What has changed? |
{:class="table table-single table-narrow"}

Each review should end with a clear delivery confidence rating, such as Green, Amber/Green, Amber, Amber/Red or Red, plus prioritised recommendations.

## What to Assure

| Area | What good looks like | Warning signs |
|------|----------------------|---------------|
| **Strategic fit** | Programme objectives trace to current organisational strategy | Strategy has moved on but the programme has not |
| **Benefits** | Each benefit has an owner, a baseline, a measure and a profile | Benefits only appear in the business case; nobody owns them |
| **Blueprint** | Target operating model is clear and agreed | Projects deliver outputs that do not add up to the blueprint |
| **Governance** | Decisions made at the right level, quickly, and recorded | Decisions stall or are made outside the board |
| **Dependencies** | Cross-project dependencies are mapped and managed | Projects discover each other's changes late |
| **Business change** | Business change managers are in post and engaged | Change is "handed over" at go-live with no readiness work |
| **Finance** | Forecast tracks actuals; contingency is controlled | Spend reported only by project, not across the programme |
| **Resources** | Key roles filled; shared resources scheduled | Same specialists double-booked across projects |
{:class="table table-single table-narrow"}

## Benefits Assurance

Benefits are where programmes most often fail quietly. Assurance should test that:

1. Benefits are measurable, with a baseline taken before change starts
2. Each benefit has a named owner in the business, not in the programme team
3. Benefit profiles show when and how each benefit will appear
4. Dis-benefits are recorded and managed, not hidden
5. Double counting with other programmes is ruled out
6. Measurement continues after the projects close

## Reporting Assurance Findings

Keep assurance reports short and decision-focused:

| Section | Content |
|---------|---------|
| **Delivery confidence** | Overall rating and a one-paragraph reason |
| **Key findings** | Three to five findings, each with evidence |
| **Recommendations** | Prioritised as critical (do now), essential (do soon) or recommended |
| **Action owners and dates** | Who will act and by when |
| **Previous actions** | Status of actions from the last review |
{:class="table table-single table-narrow"}

Findings go to the SRO first, then to the programme board. Track the actions in the programme's [action tracker](/projects/action_tracker) until closed.

## Programme Assurance Checklist

### At programme start
- [ ] Assurance strategy agreed with the SRO?
- [ ] Assurance plan scheduled against tranches?
- [ ] Independent reviewers identified and booked?
- [ ] Links to project assurance agreed?

### During delivery
- [ ] Tranche end reviews held before each go or no-go decision?
- [ ] Benefits measured against baseline?
- [ ] Review actions tracked to closure?
- [ ] Assurance plan updated when risk changes?

### At closure
- [ ] Final benefits realisation review planned?
- [ ] Lessons from assurance captured for future programmes?
