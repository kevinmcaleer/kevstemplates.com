From the UK's Behavioural Insights Team ("the Nudge Unit"). Four principles, and its whole virtue is that a room of non-specialists can use it in twenty minutes. Where [COM-B and the Behaviour Change Wheel](/behavioural-change/com-b-and-the-behaviour-change-wheel/) gives you *diagnosis*, EAST gives you *tactics*.

---

## E — Make it Easy

The most powerful and most neglected. Effort is a far bigger deterrent than people believe.

- **Harness defaults.** The pre-set option wins, overwhelmingly. If the new process isn't the default, you have already lost. *This is the highest-leverage move in almost every tech rollout.*
- **Reduce friction.** Every extra click, form field, approval or login costs you adoption. Count them. Remove three.
- **Simplify the message.** One action, stated plainly. "Log the call before you leave the car park," not a 600-word policy update.

> Ask in every design session: **"What would we have to do to make the wrong behaviour harder than the right one?"**

## A — Make it Attractive

- **Attract attention.** Personalisation, novelty, images, the person's own name. Generic all-staff emails are wallpaper.
- **Design rewards and sanctions for maximum effect.** Small, immediate and certain beats large, distant and probable. A same-day thank-you outperforms an annual bonus for habit formation.
- Frame around what *they* get, not what the organisation gets. "Saves you the Friday report" beats "improves data quality."

## S — Make it Social

The lever most under-used in corporate change, and the cheapest.

- **Show that most people perform the desired behaviour.** Descriptive norms are potent — *"78% of your region logged every call last week"*. See [Social Norms and Influence](/behavioural-change/social-norms-and-influence/).
- **Use the power of networks.** Peers, not hierarchy. See [Champion Network Playbook](/behavioural-change/champion-network-playbook/).
- **Encourage commitments to others.** A public, specific, written commitment to named colleagues dramatically outperforms a private intention.

<div class="bct-callout bct-warning" markdown="1">
**The backfiring norm**

Never advertise low compliance. "Only 30% of teams are using the new system" tells the other 70% that not using it is normal — and compliance falls. If the number is bad, use a **trend** ("up from 12% to 30% in three weeks") or a **local** comparison where the number is good. This mistake is made constantly in adoption dashboards.
</div>


## T — Make it Timely

- **Prompt people when they are most receptive.** Moments of disruption — a new role, a team move, a system change, returning from leave — are when habits are already loose. A rollout is itself such a moment: use it, don't waste it.
- **Consider immediate costs and benefits.** People heavily discount the future. Bring one benefit forward into week one, even a small one.
- **Help people plan their response.** *Implementation intentions* — "when X happens, I will do Y" — are one of the most robust findings in the field. Get people to write the sentence down. It takes 90 seconds and materially raises follow-through.

## The 20-minute EAST sprint

Run this whenever a group is stuck. Time-boxed, four rounds of five minutes.

| Round | Prompt | Output |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "How could we make this so easy it's almost automatic?" | 5+ ideas |
| 2 | "What's in it for the person doing it, right now, this week?" | 3+ ideas |
| 3 | "Who do they look to? How do we make the right thing visibly normal?" | 3+ ideas |
| 4 | "What's the exact moment this behaviour has to happen? What's already happening then?" | The cue |
{:class="table table-single table-narrow"}

Then filter with APEASE from [COM-B and the Behaviour Change Wheel](/behavioural-change/com-b-and-the-behaviour-change-wheel/) and log the survivors in [Nudge Library](/behavioural-change/nudge-library/).

## The honest limits of EAST

Say these out loud before an exec over-applies it:

1. **Nudges have modest effect sizes.** They are real but small. A well-designed nudge might move a behaviour a few percentage points — genuinely worth having, and nowhere near enough on its own for a major transformation.
2. **A nudge cannot fix a broken design.** If the new process is genuinely worse for the user, no amount of clever framing rescues it. Resistance to a bad system is the correct response — see [Resistance Decoder](/behavioural-change/resistance-decoder/).
3. **It doesn't address power, politics or grief.** Restructures need [Bridges Transition Model](/behavioural-change/bridges-transition-model/), not better emails.
4. **Manipulation is noticed.** Corporate populations are cynical and well-networked. Anything that feels like a trick costs you trust that you will need later. Nudge in the open.
