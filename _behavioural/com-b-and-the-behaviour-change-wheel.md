**The workhorse of this toolkit.** Developed by Susan Michie and colleagues at UCL. If you learn one framework, learn this one — it's the only widely-used model that connects *diagnosis* to *intervention choice* rather than leaving you to guess.

---

## The core claim

For any behaviour to happen, three things must be present:

**B = f(C, O, M)**

| | Sub-type | Plain English | Sounds like |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Capability** | Physical | Skills, strength, stamina | "I've never used anything like it" |
| | Psychological | Knowledge, mental capacity, decision-making | "I don't understand when I'm supposed to use it" |
| **Opportunity** | Physical | Environment, time, resources, access | "I don't have a licence" / "there isn't time" |
| | Social | Norms, what peers do, cultural cues | "Nobody on my team bothers" |
| **Motivation** | Reflective | Plans, beliefs, intentions, evaluations | "I don't think it's better than the old way" |
| | Automatic | Habit, emotion, impulse, desire | "I just do it the old way without thinking" |
{:class="table table-single table-narrow"}

**Remove any one and the behaviour stops.** This is why training-only rollouts fail: they fix Capability while Opportunity and Motivation are untouched.

---

## Why this matters when advising leaders

Executives default to two interventions: **tell them** (Education) and **train them** (Training). Both target Capability. In most tech and process rollouts, Capability is the *smallest* barrier. The real blockers are:

- **Physical Opportunity** — the new process takes 4 minutes longer and nobody removed anything from the day
- **Social Opportunity** — their manager still accepts the old format
- **Automatic Motivation** — 11 years of muscle memory

Bringing a leader a COM-B breakdown reframes "our people are resistant" into "we've built a system that makes the right thing harder." That's a much better conversation, and it puts the fix inside the leader's control.

## The nine intervention functions

Once you know the barrier, this tells you what *kind* of intervention can work.

| Function | What it does | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Education** | Increase knowledge/understanding | Psych. Capability |
| **Persuasion** | Stimulate feeling to prompt action | Reflective + Automatic Motivation |
| **Incentivisation** | Create expectation of reward | Reflective + Automatic Motivation |
| **Coercion** | Create expectation of punishment/cost | Motivation (use sparingly — see below) |
| **Training** | Impart skills | Physical + Psych. Capability |
| **Restriction** | Rules limiting the alternative | Physical + Social Opportunity |
| **Environmental restructuring** | Change the physical/social context | Physical + Social Opportunity, Automatic Motivation |
| **Modelling** | Provide an example to imitate | Social Opportunity, Automatic Motivation |
| **Enablement** | Increase means / reduce barriers beyond the above | Anything |
{:class="table table-single table-narrow"}

### The mapping shortcut

```
Physical Capability      → Training, Enablement
Psychological Capability → Education, Training, Enablement
Physical Opportunity     → Restriction, Environmental restructuring, Enablement
Social Opportunity       → Restriction, Env. restructuring, Modelling, Enablement
Reflective Motivation    → Education, Persuasion, Incentivisation, Coercion
Automatic Motivation     → Persuasion, Incentivisation, Coercion, Env. restructuring, Modelling, Enablement
```

Pin this. It is the single most useful table in the toolkit — go straight from your diagnosis to a shortlist.

<div class="bct-callout bct-warning" markdown="1">
**On Coercion**

It works, fast, and it is almost always the wrong first move in a workplace culture change. It produces compliance without commitment, kills discretionary effort, and teaches people to hide problems. Reserve it for genuine safety or regulatory non-negotiables, and only *after* you've made the right behaviour easy. If a leader reaches for it early, that's usually a signal they've skipped diagnosis.
</div>

## The seven policy categories

The organisational levers that deliver the functions: **communication/marketing · guidelines · fiscal measures · regulation · legislation · environmental/social planning · service provision.**

In a corporate setting, translate as: comms, policy & SOPs, budget & incentives, mandated controls, contractual terms, workspace & system design, and what the shared services function actually offers.

## APEASE — the filter that kills bad ideas politely

Score every candidate intervention 1–5:

| Criterion | The question |
|---|---|
| **A**cceptability | Will the people affected — and the stakeholders — tolerate this? |
| **P**racticality | Can we actually deliver it with the people and time we have? |
| **E**ffectiveness | Is there evidence or reason to think it will move the behaviour? |
| **A**ffordability | Can we fund it, including the ongoing cost? |
| **S**ide-effects / spillover | What else does this change? What gets worse? |
| **E**quity | Does it disadvantage any group, shift or site? |
{:class="table table-single table-narrow"}

<div class="bct-callout bct-tip" markdown="1">
**Use APEASE as a political tool**

When an exec has a pet intervention you think is weak, don't argue with it. Score it against APEASE alongside three others in front of the group. The framework does the disagreeing for you, and the conversation stays about criteria rather than about you.
</div>

## Worked example — new CRM rollout

**Behaviour:** Account managers log every client call in the CRM within 24 hours.

| Component | Finding | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Physical Capability | All have laptops, can type | ✅ Fine |
| Psych. Capability | Know how, but unclear what counts as a "call" | ⚠️ Minor gap |
| Physical Opportunity | Logging takes 6 min; no time removed from the day | 🔴 **Major** |
| Social Opportunity | Top-billing AM openly refuses; team follows | 🔴 **Major** |
| Reflective Motivation | Believe it's surveillance, not sales support | 🔴 **Major** |
| Automatic Motivation | 9 years of a spreadsheet habit | ⚠️ Real |
{:class="table table-single table-narrow"}

**Wrong response:** more training, more emails, a dashboard of shame.

**COM-B-led response:**
1. Env. restructuring — cut the form to 4 fields; mobile voice-to-text; auto-populate from calendar (Physical Opportunity)
2. Modelling — get the top-billing AM into the design, not the firing line; publish *their* pipeline gains (Social Opportunity)
3. Persuasion + Enablement — the CRM feeds *them* a warm-lead list; reframe from reporting to receiving (Reflective Motivation)
4. Education — 1-page "what counts" card (Psych. Capability)
5. Env. restructuring — the Monday pipeline meeting runs off the CRM only. No CRM entry, no discussion. (Automatic Motivation — new cue)

Note that #5 does more than any amount of comms, and costs nothing.

## When this is the wrong tool

- **When the problem is emotional, not practical** — a restructure where people are grieving. Use [Bridges Transition Model](/behavioural-change/bridges-transition-model/).
- **When you need a 3-minute exec pitch** — COM-B is too clinical. Use [Switch — Rider Elephant Path](/behavioural-change/switch-rider-elephant-path/).
- **When one individual is stuck** — use [ADKAR — Individual Change](/behavioural-change/adkar-individual-change/).
