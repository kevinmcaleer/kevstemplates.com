The peer layer between "leadership says" and "I do". Most programmes invest in leadership comms and central training and skip this entirely — which is where the influence actually lives.

---

## Who to pick

**Look for:** people others already go to for help · respected for competence not seniority · already frustrated with the current way · socially connected across teams · a bit sceptical *(the converted sceptic is your most credible advocate)*.

**Avoid:** volunteers with spare capacity *(usually a signal)* · the manager's nominee where the manager just wants them out of the way · people who are enthusiastic about everything · anyone universally regarded as a company mouthpiece.

**Find them with the two-question sociogram** in [Diffusion of Innovations](/behavioural-change/diffusion-of-innovations/): *"Who do you ask when you're not sure how to do something here?"* and *"Whose opinion on this would you actually take seriously?"*

**Coverage, not quantity.** One per team or shift beats twenty in head office. A night shift with no champion has no champion.

---

## The ask — be specific

Vague asks get vague effort. Use this:

> "I'd like you to be a champion for `[change]`. Concretely that means:
> - Being one of the first to use it, so you can speak from experience
> - Being the person on your team people ask, so they don't have to raise a ticket
> - **Telling me what's not working — including things I won't want to hear**
>
> It's about `[2 hours a week for 8 weeks]`. You'll get `[early access / direct line to me / your name on what changes / it goes in your development record]`.
>
> I'm asking you because `[specific, true reason]`."

⚠️ **Get their manager's agreement to the time, in advance and in writing.** A champion whose day job doesn't shrink will quietly stop, and you'll wrongly conclude they weren't committed.

---

## What champions are for

| ✅ Yes | ❌ No |
|---|---|
| Being the visible early user | Being unpaid trainers for a syllabus they didn't write |
| Answering peer questions informally | Policing compliance |
| Feeding back what's broken | Defending decisions they had no part in |
| Testing changes before wider release | Reporting on colleagues |
| Being named in what changed | Being the sponsor's mouthpiece |
{:class="table table-single table-narrow"}

⚠️ **Never ask a champion to police.** The moment they become enforcement, they lose the peer status that made them useful — and they can't get it back.

## The rhythm

| When | What | Length |
|---|---|---|
| Kick-off | Why, what, and the ask. Together, in a room if possible. | 90 min |
| Weekly (first 6 wks) | "What's broken?" call. **You listen; you don't update.** | 30 min |
| Fortnightly after | Same | 30 min |
| Monthly | Publish what changed because of them | — |
{:class="table table-single table-narrow"}

<div class="bct-callout bct-warning" markdown="1">
**The weekly call must not become a status update**

The moment you start using it to broadcast programme news, champions stop bringing problems and start attending politely. Protect its purpose ruthlessly. Ask three questions and then be quiet: *What's broken? What are people saying when I'm not there? What should we change?*
</div>

## Keeping them

Champion networks decay in about six weeks unless actively fed. The four things that keep them:

1. **Visible influence.** Publish the list of changes made because a champion raised it, with names. Nothing else comes close for retention — it proves the role is real.
2. **Information first.** They hear things before their peers. Cheap and highly valued.
3. **Access.** A direct line to you and, occasionally, to the sponsor. A sponsor spending 30 minutes with the champion network is one of the highest-value uses of their time.
4. **Recognition their manager sees.** A note to their manager is worth more than a certificate.

## Winding down

Champion networks should end deliberately, not fade. At around day 90:

- Publish what the network changed, in full
- Thank them specifically and in writing to their managers
- Ask who wants to stay involved as the behaviour moves into business as usual
- **Name the ongoing owner in the line.** If nobody owns it after the network stands down, drift starts immediately — see [Drift Check — 30 60 90](/behavioural-change/drift-check-30-60-90/).
