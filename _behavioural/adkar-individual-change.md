From Prosci. Its value isn't as a programme plan — it's as a **diagnostic for an individual**. Change happens one person at a time, and each person is stuck at exactly one step. Identify that step and you know what to do.

---

## The five sequential steps

| Step | The person's internal state | Stuck here means | What actually helps |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A**wareness | *"I understand why this change is happening"* | They've heard the announcement but not the reason | The business case, in their language; **their manager**, not a town hall |
| **D**esire | *"I've decided to participate"* | They get it and still won't | WIIFM, involvement in design, addressing the personal cost honestly |
| **K**nowledge | *"I know how to change"* | Willing but doesn't know how | Training, job aids, coaching |
| **A**bility | *"I can actually do it in my job"* | Knows in the classroom, fails at the desk | Practice, time, support at the moment of use, removing obstacles |
| **R**einforcement | *"It's sticking"* | Did it for three weeks, reverted | Recognition, measurement, consequences, system defaults |
{:class="table table-single table-narrow"}

**The order is strict.** You cannot fix a Desire gap with more Knowledge — but that's exactly what organisations do. When people resist, we send more training. It never works, and it's expensive.

---

## The diagnostic question set

Ask any individual, in order. Stop at the first "no" — that's your barrier.

1. Can you tell me **why** we're making this change? *(Awareness)*
2. What's your **personal view** on it — do you want it to happen? *(Desire)*
3. Do you know **how** to do the new thing? *(Knowledge)*
4. When you sat down to actually do it, what happened? *(Ability)*
5. What would make you **keep** doing it in six months? *(Reinforcement)*

Score 1–5 per step. Anything ≤3 is the barrier. Don't work on later steps until it's cleared.

---

## Rolled up to a group

Sample 10–15 people across roles and score each. The pattern tells you where the programme is failing:

| Pattern | Meaning | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Low A across the board | Comms failed or never reached the line | Managers cascade, not email — [Manager Conversation Guide](/behavioural-change/manager-conversation-guide/) |
| High A, low D | They understand and object | You have a real design or trust problem — [Resistance Decoder](/behavioural-change/resistance-decoder/) |
| High D, low K | Enthusiasm outrunning enablement | Training, job aids |
| High K, low A(bility) | Classic training-without-support | Floorwalkers, protected practice time, [Nudge Library](/behavioural-change/nudge-library/) |
| Everything high, low R | You're 90 days from full reversion | [Embedding and Reinforcement](/behavioural-change/embedding-and-reinforcement/) urgently |
{:class="table table-single table-narrow"}

<div class="bct-callout bct-tip" markdown="1">
**For senior leaders**

ADKAR is the easiest framework to get an exec to actually use, because it's five words and they can apply it to themselves. Ask a leadership team to privately score *themselves* on the change. The spread in the room is usually the most revealing data you'll get all quarter — and it's often a Desire gap at the top, which nobody has said aloud.
</div>

## Where ADKAR is weak

- It's **linear and individual** — it says little about systems, incentives, power or the physical environment. Pair with [COM-B and the Behaviour Change Wheel](/behavioural-change/com-b-and-the-behaviour-change-wheel/).
- It can encourage a checkbox mentality ("Awareness — done, we sent the email").
- It doesn't tell you *what* intervention to use, only *where* the gap is.
