## Primary frameworks

**COM-B and the Behaviour Change Wheel** — Susan Michie, Lou Atkins and Robert West, *The Behaviour Change Wheel: A Guide to Designing Interventions* (Silverback, 2014). Developed at UCL from a synthesis of 19 existing behaviour change frameworks. The most rigorous diagnosis-to-intervention model available, and the backbone of this toolkit. UCL's Centre for Behaviour Change publishes free resources.
→ [UCL Behaviour Change resources](https://www.ucl.ac.uk/behaviour-change/resources/advancing-behavioural-science) · [ModelThinkers summary](https://modelthinkers.com/mental-model/com-b-behaviour-change-wheel)

**EAST** — Behavioural Insights Team, *EAST: Four Simple Ways to Apply Behavioural Insights* (2014, updated 2024). Free PDF, about 50 pages, the single best practical read on this list.
→ [BIT publication](https://www.bi.team/publications/east-four-simple-ways-to-apply-behavioural-insights/) · [OECD OPSI summary](https://oecd-opsi.org/toolkits/east-four-simple-ways-to-apply-behavioural-insights/) · [The Decision Lab reference](https://thedecisionlab.com/reference-guide/management/east-framework)

**ADKAR** — Jeff Hiatt / Prosci. Prosci's ongoing benchmarking research is the main source for the finding that active and visible sponsorship is the largest single contributor to change success.
→ [Prosci on the primary sponsor's role](https://www.prosci.com/blog/primary-sponsors-role-and-importance) · [Prosci change management success](https://www.prosci.com/change-management-success)

**Transitions** — William Bridges, *Managing Transitions: Making the Most of Change* (4th ed., 2016). Short, humane, and the best thing written on the emotional side of organisational change.

**Switch** — Chip and Dan Heath, *Switch: How to Change Things When Change Is Hard* (2010). The most readable, and the best source of language for talking to executives.

**Diffusion of Innovations** — Everett Rogers (5th ed., 2003). Dense but foundational.

**Nudge / choice architecture** — Richard Thaler and Cass Sunstein, *Nudge: The Final Edition* (2021). The final edition is notably more measured about effect sizes than the original, which is itself instructive.

**Habits** — Wendy Wood, *Good Habits, Bad Habits* (2019). The research-grounded one; stronger than the popular alternatives on the role of context and friction.

## On the "70% failure" myth

The statistic has no reliable empirical basis. It traces to 1990s business-process-reengineering consulting material and was never supported by peer-reviewed evidence.

- Mark Hughes, "Do 70 Per Cent of All Organizational Change Initiatives Really Fail?", *Journal of Change Management* (2011) — the standard academic critique.
→ [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/233202794_Do_70_Per_Cent_of_All_Organizational_Change_Initiatives_Really_Fail)
- Accessible summary of the origins and the counter-evidence: [Debunking the myth](https://deepimpactonline.com/70-percent-of-change-initiatives-fail/)

Where outcomes have been examined properly, most initiatives land in the middle — partial success — with outright failure a minority. The practical implication: change usually **half-lands**, and closing that gap is the job. → [Myths and Traps in Change Management](/behavioural-change/myths-and-traps-in-change-management/)

## Worth knowing about, with caveats

**Kotter's 8 Steps** — the most widely known change model, and useful as a checklist of things not to forget. Weak as a diagnostic: it tells you what to do, not what's wrong. Best treated as a governance sequence rather than a behavioural tool.

**Lewin's Unfreeze–Change–Refreeze** — historically important, and a poor fit for continuous change. [Force Field Analysis](/behavioural-change/force-field-analysis/), from the same author, has aged far better and is still genuinely useful.

**Learning styles and MBTI-style typing** — both common in change collateral, both weakly supported. Matching instruction to "learning styles" has repeatedly failed to show benefit; MBTI has known reliability and validity problems. Don't build credibility on either. → [Myths and Traps in Change Management](/behavioural-change/myths-and-traps-in-change-management/)

## If you only read three things

1. **EAST** (BIT) — free, 50 pages, immediately usable
2. **Managing Transitions** (Bridges) — for the human side you'll otherwise miss
3. **Switch** (Heath) — for the language to use with senior leaders

## Note on how this toolkit uses sources

Frameworks here are adapted for a corporate change context rather than reproduced verbatim — most originate in public health or public policy, where the populations, incentives and ethics differ. Where the adaptation is a stretch, the note says so. If you're presenting any of this as evidence-based to a sceptical audience, go to the primary source first; the summaries here are working tools, not citations.
