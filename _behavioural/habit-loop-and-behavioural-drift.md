Roughly 40% of daily workplace actions are habitual rather than decided. You are not competing with people's opinions — you are competing with their **automaticity**.

---

## The loop

```
   CUE  ──────►  ROUTINE  ──────►  REWARD
    ▲                                 │
    └──────────  CRAVING  ◄───────────┘
```

- **Cue** — the trigger. Time, place, preceding event, emotional state, other people.
- **Routine** — the behaviour.
- **Reward** — what the brain gets. Relief, completion, status, avoiding effort.

**You cannot delete a habit. You can only replace the routine while keeping the cue and reward.** This is why "stop using the spreadsheet" fails and "when the call ends, log it on your phone — and get your warm-lead list back" works.

## Designing a new workplace habit

**Habit design**

**Behaviour:** `_______________`
**Existing cue we can hijack:** `_______________`
*(What already reliably happens right before the moment this behaviour should occur? Monday stand-up, end of a call, opening the laptop, ticket closing.)*
**Old routine it replaces:** `_______________`
**Old reward we must still deliver:** `_______________`
**New reward, within 24 hours:** `_______________`
**Implementation intention:** "When `_____________`, I will `_____________`."
</div>


⚠️ **Attach to an existing cue.** New behaviours anchored to new cues almost never stick — you'd be building two habits at once. Find something already automatic and bolt on.

⚠️ **The reward must be fast.** "This will improve forecasting accuracy next quarter" is not a reward the automatic system can register. "The system now pre-fills your Friday report" is.

## Why adoption graphs look like this

```
Adoption
 100% │      ╭─╮
      │     ╱   ╲___
      │    ╱        ╲______        ← the true baseline
      │   ╱                ╲_____
      │  ╱
   0% └──────────────────────────────► weeks
       1   2   3   4   6   8   12
         ↑           ↑
      novelty +   attention
      supervision   leaves
```

The early spike is not adoption. It is **novelty plus surveillance**. Everyone knows they're being watched in week one. The real number is what you see at week 12 when nobody is looking.

**Practical consequences:**
1. **Never report week-2 adoption as success.** You'll be held to a number that was always going to fall, and the fall will read as failure.
2. **Set the drift check dates before go-live** — see [Drift Check — 30 60 90](/behavioural-change/drift-check-30-60-90/).
3. **Plan a deliberate week-8 re-intervention.** Most programmes have disbanded by then. That's precisely when reinforcement is worth most.

## The four causes of drift

| Cause | Sign | Counter |
|---|---|---|
| **Attention withdrawal** | Adoption falls the week the programme team stands down | Hand to the line *with* a named owner and a live metric before you leave |
| **Effort accumulation** | "It's fine normally but not when we're busy" | The new way must be easier than the old *on the worst day*, not the average day |
| **Reward extinction** | Early recognition stops, behaviour stops | Move recognition into the standing management rhythm, not the programme |
| **Competing change** | A new priority lands and displaces it | Sequence changes; show the sponsor the full list of what's live |
{:class="table table-single table-narrow"}

**Effort accumulation is the killer.** Test your process at peak load, understaffed, on a Friday. If it fails then, it will fail permanently — because the exception becomes the excuse, and the excuse becomes the norm.

## Making it structural

The most reliable form of reinforcement isn't motivational, it's structural. Get the behaviour into:

- **System defaults and mandatory fields** — the strongest lever available
- **The standing meeting agenda** — if it isn't discussed, it isn't real
- **Onboarding** — new joiners should never learn the old way exists
- **Objectives and performance conversations**
- **Whatever the leadership team asks about** — organisations optimise for what gets asked

> The blunt version for a sponsor: **the behaviour will last exactly as long as someone senior keeps asking about it.** Design who that is, and put it in their standing agenda.
