# Identify Benefits

Benefits identification ensures that projects deliver measurable value to the organisation and that this value is tracked and realised.

---

## What is a Benefit?

<div class="alert alert-info">
<i class="fas fa-info-circle mr-2"></i>
<strong>Definition:</strong> A benefit is a measurable improvement resulting from a project outcome, perceived as positive by one or more stakeholders.
</div>

---

## Types of Benefits

| Type | Description | Examples |
|------|-------------|----------|
| **Financial** | Direct monetary impact | Cost savings, revenue increase |
| **Efficiency** | Process improvements | Time savings, reduced errors |
| **Quality** | Improved outputs | Customer satisfaction, accuracy |
| **Risk reduction** | Mitigated threats | Compliance, security |
| **Strategic** | Competitive advantage | Market position, capability |
{:class="table table-single table-narrow"}

## Key Questions

For each benefit, answer:
- What is the benefit?
- How will it be measured?
- What is the baseline (current state)?
- What is the target?
- When will it be realised?
- Who owns realisation?
- What dependencies exist?

| Field | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Benefit ID** | Unique identifier |

| **Target** | Expected improvement |
| **Owner** | Person accountable for realisation |
| **Realisation date** | When benefit will be achieved |

## Outputs, Outcomes and Benefits

Projects deliver outputs; the business uses them to change how it works; that change produces benefits. Keeping the three apart stops a project claiming a benefit just because it delivered something.

<div class="mermaid">
flowchart LR
    A[Output<br/>new CRM system] --> B[Capability<br/>sales team can use it]
    B --> C[Outcome<br/>quotes issued faster]
    C --> D[Benefit<br/>15% more orders won]
    D --> E[Strategic<br/>objective: growth]
    classDef blue fill:#108BB9,stroke:none,color:#fff
    class A,B,C,D,E blue
</div>

| Term | Meaning | Who delivers it |
|------|---------|-----------------|
| **Output** | A product the project hands over | Project team |
| **Capability** | What the output makes possible | Project and business |
| **Outcome** | The changed state once people use the capability | Business |
| **Benefit** | The measurable improvement from the outcome | Business, owned by a named person |
{:class="table table-single table-narrow"}

---

## Benefits Mapping

A benefits map traces each project output through to the strategic objectives it supports. Build it in a workshop with the sponsor and senior user:

1. Start from the strategic objectives the project supports
2. Work back to the benefits that would show progress towards them
3. Identify the outcomes and business changes each benefit depends on
4. Link each outcome to the project outputs that enable it
5. Look for outputs that link to no benefit, and benefits with no enabling output

Outputs with no benefit are candidates for removal from scope. Benefits with no output need either a new output or a business change owned outside the project.

## Writing Measurable Benefits

| Weak | Measurable |
|------|-----------|
| Better customer service | Average call handling time falls from 8 to 6 minutes by Q3 |
| Improved efficiency | Invoice processing cost falls from £4.20 to £2.50 per invoice |
| Reduced risk | Audit findings rated high fall from 6 to 0 at next annual audit |
| Happier staff | Staff survey engagement score rises from 62% to 70% within a year |
{:class="table table-single table-narrow"}

Take the baseline **before** the change starts. A benefit with no baseline cannot be proved later.

---

## Benefit Profiles

Each significant benefit gets a short profile, reviewed by its owner:

| Field | Description |
|-------|-------------|
| **Description** | What will improve, in business terms |
| **Owner** | The business manager accountable for realising it |
| **Measure and source** | Exactly how it is measured, and where the data comes from |
| **Baseline** | Value and date of the current measurement |
| **Target and profile** | Target value, and how it builds up over time |
| **Dependencies** | Outputs, business changes and other projects it relies on |
| **Dis-benefits** | Negative effects that come with it |
| **Attribution** | How much of the change is down to this project |
{:class="table table-single table-narrow"}

---

## Dis-benefits

A dis-benefit is a measurable negative effect of the change, seen by at least one stakeholder, such as extra data entry for one team or the cost of running a new system. Record and own dis-benefits like benefits. Hiding them damages trust when they appear after go-live.

---

## Common Pitfalls

| Pitfall | What to do instead |
|---------|--------------------|
| Benefits written to justify a decision already made | Identify benefits before choosing the solution |
| The project manager owns the benefits | Benefits belong to business owners who will be there after the project closes |
| Counting the same saving in two business cases | Check with the PMO or portfolio office for overlaps |
| No baseline | Measure the current state during initiation |
| Benefits only reviewed at closure | Plan reviews after closure, when most benefits appear; see [Benefits Review](/projects/benefits_review) |
{:class="table table-single table-narrow"}

---

## Benefits Identification Checklist

- [ ] Benefits mapped to strategic objectives?
- [ ] Each benefit measurable, with a baseline?
- [ ] Each benefit has a named business owner?
- [ ] Dis-benefits recorded?
- [ ] Benefits in the business case match the benefits register?
- [ ] Reviews scheduled after the project closes?
