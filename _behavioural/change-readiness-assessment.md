Score honestly. A low score isn't a reason not to proceed — it's a list of what to fix first, and a defensible piece of evidence when you need to tell a leader the date is wrong.

Score each 1 (not at all) to 5 (fully true).

<div class="bct-callout bct-tip" markdown="1">
**Score it on screen**

The [interactive version](/behavioural-change/readiness-tool/) does the weighting and the thresholds for you, applies the stop signals, and tells you how to present the result — including which section to lead with, which is the part that decides whether a bad score gets heard or argued with.

## A · Sponsorship — weight ×3

| # | Statement | Score |
|---|---|---|
| A1 | There is one named senior sponsor, not a committee | `_` |
| A2 | The sponsor can explain the change in their own words, without notes | `_` |
| A3 | The sponsor has done something visible about it in the last month | `_` |
| A4 | The sponsor's peers are publicly aligned — no visible dissent at that level | `_` |
| A5 | The sponsor will still be in post in 12 months | `_` |
| | **Subtotal /25 → ×3 =** | `___` |
{:class="table table-single table-narrow"}

Weighted heaviest because active, visible sponsorship is repeatedly identified as the single largest contributor to change success. **If A is under 15, fix this before anything else** — see [Sponsor Briefing Pack](/behavioural-change/sponsor-briefing-pack/).

## C · Capacity and saturation

| # | Statement | Score |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | The affected teams are not already absorbing a major change | `_` |
| C2 | Managers have time to lead this, not just forward emails | `_` |
| C3 | There is resource for the sustain phase, not just go-live | `_` |
| C4 | Nothing significant lands on these teams in the next quarter | `_` |
| C5 | We know how many other initiatives are live for these people | `_` |
| | **Subtotal /25** | `___` |
{:class="table table-single table-narrow"}

<div class="bct-callout bct-warning" markdown="1">
**Change saturation**

If C is under 12, the most valuable advice you can give a senior leader is **sequence or stop something**. Bring the list of live initiatives affecting these teams — most leadership teams have never seen it assembled in one place, and it is often genuinely shocking. This is one of the highest-value single artefacts you can produce.
</div>


## D · Trust and history

| # | Statement | Score |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | The last major change here was seen as successful by the people affected | `_` |
| D2 | Commitments made during previous changes were kept | `_` |
| D3 | People believe they can raise concerns without cost | `_` |
| D4 | This isn't a rebrand of something that was tried before and failed | `_` |
| D5 | The reason for the change is credible, not obviously a cover story | `_` |
| | **Subtotal /25** | `___` |
{:class="table table-single table-narrow"}

Low D is the hardest to fix and the most often ignored. If D is low, **acknowledge the history out loud, early**. "We know the 2023 rollout was painful and we know what we got wrong" buys more credibility than any benefits statement. Pretending it didn't happen tells people you either weren't paying attention or think they weren't.

## E · Design quality

| # | Statement | Score |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | The new way is genuinely better *for the person doing it*, not just for the org | `_` |
| E2 | People who do the work were involved in designing it | `_` |
| E3 | It has been tested under realistic pressure, not just demoed | `_` |
| E4 | The new way is easier than the old way at the point of use | `_` |
| E5 | We can make the new way the default | `_` |
| | **Subtotal /25** | `___` |
{:class="table table-single table-narrow"}

If E1 or E4 is 1–2, you have a design problem masquerading as a change problem. **Say this before launch.** Afterwards, it will be read as excuse-making.

## Scoring

<div class="bct-callout bct-note" markdown="1">
**Totals**

A (×3) `___` + B `___` + C `___` + D `___` + E `___` = **`___` / 175**
</div>


| Score | Read |
|---|---|
| 140+ | Genuinely ready. Move. |
| 105–139 | Proceed, with named fixes for your two weakest sections |
| 70–104 | Do not launch yet. You'd be spending credibility to buy a date. |
| Under 70 | The honest conversation is about scope, sequence or timing |
{:class="table table-single table-narrow"}

**Any single section under 10 is a stop signal regardless of total.** A brilliant plan with an absent sponsor fails. A perfect design landing on an exhausted team fails.

---

## How to present a bad score

Never lead with the total — it invites an argument about the instrument rather than the substance.

1. Lead with the **strongest** section. It's real, and it establishes you're not just being negative.
2. Present the weakest section as **a decision to make**, not a verdict: *"On current sponsorship visibility, we'd be launching without the biggest single success factor. Two options: we build that in over three weeks, or we go on the date and accept a materially lower landing. Which do you want?"*
3. Have the three-week fix ready. Bringing a problem without a route through it is what makes people stop inviting you.
