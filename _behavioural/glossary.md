Plain-English definitions. Useful when a leader uses a term loosely, or when you need to be precise without sounding academic.

---

**ADKAR** — Awareness, Desire, Knowledge, Ability, Reinforcement. A five-step model of individual change; most useful as a diagnostic for where one person is stuck. → [ADKAR — Individual Change](/behavioural-change/adkar-individual-change/)

**APEASE** — Acceptability, Practicality, Effectiveness, Affordability, Side-effects, Equity. The filter for choosing between candidate interventions. → [COM-B and the Behaviour Change Wheel](/behavioural-change/com-b-and-the-behaviour-change-wheel/)

**Automaticity** — the degree to which a behaviour happens without conscious decision. The end goal of embedding.

**Behaviour** — an observable action by a named person at a specified moment. If you can't film it, it isn't one. → [Target Behaviour Definition](/behavioural-change/target-behaviour-definition/)

**Bright spot** — a place where the desired behaviour is already happening despite the same constraints. The cheapest source of solutions and the most credible evidence.

**Change saturation** — the point at which cumulative concurrent change exceeds a group's absorptive capacity, degrading everything running.

**COM-B** — Capability, Opportunity, Motivation → Behaviour. The diagnostic backbone of this toolkit. → [COM-B and the Behaviour Change Wheel](/behavioural-change/com-b-and-the-behaviour-change-wheel/)

**Counter-measure** — a metric watched to catch the thing that might get worse while your headline metric improves.

**Cue** — the trigger that fires a habit. Time, place, preceding event, emotional state, other people.

**Descriptive norm** — what people actually do. Generally more powerful than injunctive norms for routine behaviours. → [Social Norms and Influence](/behavioural-change/social-norms-and-influence/)

**Diffusion** — how an innovation spreads through a population over time, from innovators to laggards. → [Diffusion of Innovations](/behavioural-change/diffusion-of-innovations/)

**Drift** — the gradual reversion of a behaviour after attention withdraws. Typically visible from week six. → [Habit Loop and Behavioural Drift](/behavioural-change/habit-loop-and-behavioural-drift/)

**EAST** — Easy, Attractive, Social, Timely. Four practical principles for intervention design. → [EAST — Easy Attractive Social Timely](/behavioural-change/east-easy-attractive-social-timely/)

**Effort accumulation** — the way a small per-instance cost becomes intolerable under pressure. The most common cause of drift.

**Environmental restructuring** — changing the physical or social context rather than the person. Usually the cheapest and most durable intervention type.

**Implementation intention** — a written "when X happens, I will do Y" statement. Cheap, fast, and robustly effective.

**Injunctive norm** — what people believe others approve of, as opposed to what they do.

**Intervention function** — the nine broad categories of thing an intervention can do: Education, Persuasion, Incentivisation, Coercion, Training, Restriction, Environmental restructuring, Modelling, Enablement.

**Lagging indicator** — an outcome measure that moves late. Business benefit, cycle time, cost.

**Leading indicator** — a behaviour measure that moves early. The one most programmes don't have. → [Measurement Plan — Leading and Lagging](/behavioural-change/measurement-plan-leading-and-lagging/)

**Neutral zone** — Bridges' term for the period after the old way has gone and before the new is fluent. Where performance dips and programmes get cancelled. → [Bridges Transition Model](/behavioural-change/bridges-transition-model/)

**Nudge** — a change to choice architecture that alters behaviour without forbidding options or significantly changing incentives. Real but modest effects.

**Parallel running** — operating old and new systems simultaneously. The most common single cause of stalled adoption. → [Force Field Analysis](/behavioural-change/force-field-analysis/)

**Restraining force** — something holding the current state in place. Removing one is usually cheaper and more effective than adding pressure. → [Force Field Analysis](/behavioural-change/force-field-analysis/)

**Say–do gap** — the distance between what leaders say and what they visibly do. The most damaging executive failure mode. → [Executive Failure Modes](/behavioural-change/executive-failure-modes/)

**Sponsor** — the senior person with the authority to remove organisational barriers and decide. Active, visible sponsorship is the largest single contributor to change success. → [Sponsor Briefing Pack](/behavioural-change/sponsor-briefing-pack/)

**Transition** — the psychological process people go through, as distinct from the situational change. Change happens on a date; transition takes as long as it takes.

## Terms worth pushing back on

**"Buy-in"** — an attitude, unmeasurable and slow to shift. Push the conversation to observable behaviour instead.

**"Burning platform"** — fear-based urgency. Produces short-term compliance, risk-aversion and information-hiding. Doesn't sustain.

**"Change fatigue"** — often used to dismiss a legitimate capacity problem as an attitude. Usually it's saturation, and saturation is a leadership decision.

**"Culture change"** — culture is an outcome, not a lever. Ask what behaviours would be different. → [Target Behaviour Definition](/behavioural-change/target-behaviour-definition/)

**"Resistance"** — treats a symptom as a personality trait. Decode it instead. → [Resistance Decoder](/behavioural-change/resistance-decoder/)

**"They need to be more accountable"** — almost always describes a system that makes the right behaviour costly, not a population that lacks character.
